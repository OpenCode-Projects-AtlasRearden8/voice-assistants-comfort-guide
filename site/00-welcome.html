Welcome: Smart Speakers, Explained Like a Friend Would

If you've ever shouted "Hey Alexa!" only to have the speaker do something closely related to what you asked, this guide is for you. No jargon, no condescension — just the stuff worth knowing, in the order it's actually useful.

Who this is for

You (or someone you love) has a smart speaker — an Amazon Echo with Alexa, a Google Nest speaker, or an Apple HomePod with Siri — or is thinking about one. Maybe it was a gift. Maybe it's still in the box. Maybe it's currently sitting there judging you silently. All of these are valid starting points.

How to use this guide

  • Start with Setup in One Weekend if the speaker is still new to you.
  • Skim Privacy Settings Worth Touching before you get comfortable — it takes 15 minutes and saves headaches.
  • Learn Daily Commands That Actually Earn Their Keep and keep the Command Cheat Sheets nearby.
  • Set up Routines: Reminders and Timers on Autopilot once you have the basics down.
  • Read Voice Shopping Without Getting Burned so the first thing your speaker buys isn't a pallet of who-knows-what.
  • Handle Kids and Guests before they handle you.
  • When things go wrong: My Speaker Stopped Listening.
  • And before you buy a second (or third) speaker, read When Your Phone Just Does It Better.

The one-sentence version of the whole guide

A smart speaker is just a voice remote for helpful things — set it up, lock down the few settings that matter, teach it a handful of words, and let it earn its spot on the counter.

Where this guide fits

This is a companion to the companion piece smart-home-comfort-guide. That guide covers making your home itself smart (lights, thermostats, plugs). This one covers the talking hockey puck in the kitchen. They overlap a bit — commands are mostly the same either way — but you can read either one first.

Our promise

Everything here is in plain English, step by step, and assumes zero prior knowledge. If a phrase like "voice profile" makes you want to throw the speaker out the window, we define it first (see the glossary).
