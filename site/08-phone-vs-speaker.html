When Your Phone Just Does It Better

Here's a loving truth bomb: your phone is already the world's best smart assistant. It's always within arm's reach, it has a screen, it has a keyboard, it has your thumbs. The speaker wins in exactly three situations. Everywhere else, the phone wins.

Where the speaker wins

  1. Kitchen, hands busy. Covered in flour? "Set a timer for 12 minutes" without wiping your hands. That's a real win and no phone matches it.
  2. Whole-house audio. Saying "play the news" in the living room and having it come out of the ceiling-sized speaker beats strapping an alarm to the wall.
  3. Alarms and nightstand duty. Dedicated clock hardware that isn't also your doomscrolling trap is genuinely healthier at 11pm.

The friendly rule

Use the speaker for the sentence you'd say out loud anyway. Use the phone for the sentence that involves a screen.

Examples:

  • "Timer for 20 minutes" → speaker.
  • "Order more paper towels" → speaker, if you set it up.
  • "Book a dentist appointment" → phone (you'll need to type details anyway).
  • "Text my sister I'll be late" → phone for typing, or speaker if you really trust the transcription.
  • "Remind me at 7am" → either. Speaker is nice because it's already plugged in where you'll see it.

The phone's assistant is the same assistant

"Hey Siri" on your iPhone and "Hey Google" on your Android do essentially everything the speaker does — and they travel with you. If you only ever use your phone's voice assistant and skip the countertop puck, you have lost nothing important. The speaker is a convenience, not a necessity.

Don't buy a second speaker for the bedroom

That's the single most common "do I really need this" moment. A plugged-in phone with an alarm does 95% of the job. If the second speaker arrives because of a sale, great. If it arrives because of FOMO, put the $50 in a jar instead.
