Voice Shopping Without Getting Burned

Voice shopping is the feature with the biggest gap between "wow, magic" and "why did my speaker order a hairbrush that costs $89." Set it up on purpose, keep it on a leash, and know the three classic scams.

Turn it on on purpose (or off entirely)

You have three sensible settings. Pick one, ignore the rest.

  1. Off completely. Alexa: Settings → Account Settings → Voice Purchasing → Off. Google: Assistant settings → "On voice purchases" → Off. This is the right answer for most households.
  2. PIN-protected. On, but the speaker asks for a 4-digit confirmation code every time. You say the code out loud — which means small children shouldn't know it, and you should say it quietly.
  3. Restricted to a single "buy again" list. Alexa lets you limit purchasing to your "Prime" / reorder items only. Useful if it's really all you want to voice-buy (paper towels, sure; a kayak, no).

Also check "For kids, request adult approval" where available.

Who to blame when things go sideways

  • Only you should be able to place an order. On Alexa, Family Profile members are all allowed to voice-purchase by default — turn it off for everyone but yourself (Settings → Account Settings → Family Profile → pick the person → Voice Purchasing → off).
  • Same with the kid-friendly setup in kids and guests.

Scam #1: The fake-skill / fake-action con

Someone says (email, phone call, pop-up ad): "To claim your prize, just enable this Alexa skill" — it's a skill that quietly grants permission to spend, or that asks personal info through the speaker. Paid-for prizes are fake. Never enable a skill you've never heard of, and never give account info through your speaker or a link someone sent you. Enable skills only from the official Alexa/Google apps.

Scam #2: The surprise subscription charge

"Free trial" skills (Alexa) and in-speaker purchases for kids' content can auto-renew monthly. Every so often, check:

  • Amazon → your orders → filter by digital orders, and check "Prime Mad Skills" (Alexa → Settings → Skills) for subscriptions.
  • Google → play.google.com → Payments & subscriptions.

Cancel anything you don't actively use.

Scam #3: The voice-impersonation song-and-dance

Since speakers can be made to speak for you or text/call on your behalf, someone pretending to be you (or pretending to need money from you) gets easier. The rule that fixes most of this: never trust a voice you didn't set up. Verify with a code word with your family. "If I ever call asking for money and don't say 'pineapple,' the call is fake." Tell the whole family, ideally at dinner, slightly proudly.

Quick scan of a suspicious voice order

If something odd shows up:

  1. Check the order in the app (Alexa → More → Orders; Google → Payments & subscriptions).
  2. "Cancel" is available until shipping. Say, or tap, "Cancel order" the same day. You don't need to call anyone or get scammed twice over the "return."
  3. Change the PIN / turn voice purchasing off first, then investigate. Don't argue with the speaker.

Bottom line

Voice shopping is great for one thing — reordering stuff you buy monthly. For everything else, your phone is a better checkout lane. (More on that in phone vs. speaker.)
