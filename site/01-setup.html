Setup in One Weekend

Setting up any smart speaker is a three-act play: plug it in, get it on your Wi-Fi, and teach it your voice. Here's the whole show for each brand — same order, slightly different words.

Before you start

  • Your Wi-Fi password (write it down; you'll type it twice at least once).
  • The phone or tablet you actually use daily — setup apps install on your phone, not from your computer.
  • The speaker's original box nearby. The app sometimes asks for a serial number printed there. A photo on your phone works too.

Setup checklist

A printable checklist version of this lives in setup-checklists.md. Print it, tape it to the fridge, cross things off like a normal human.

Act 1: Plug it in and open the app

  1. Plug the speaker into an outlet. Not a power strip crammed with six other things — speakers hate brownouts and so do you.
  2. Wait until it lights up or makes a cheerful noise (about 1 minute).
  3. Download the right app on your phone:
  4. Alexa: Amazon Alexa
  5. Google: Google Home
  6. Siri (HomePod): the built-in Home app — no download needed
  7. Sign in with the account that matches your speaker: your Amazon account (Alexa), your Google account (Google), or your Apple ID (HomePod). If you use Amazon for shopping anyway, same login. Nice.

Act 2: Get it on Wi-Fi

  1. Open the app. It will usually find the new speaker automatically and offer to set it up. Say yes.
  2. When it asks, put in your Wi-Fi name and password. Capital letters count. The word "password" does not appear anywhere in your password unless you put it there.
  3. Wait while it connects. The ring light goes solid or spinning, then calms down. If you feel like you've been staring at a spinning light too long, skip to troubleshooting.
  4. Pick the room it's in ("Kitchen," "Living Room"). This matters later when you say things like "turn off the kitchen light."

Act 3: Teach it your voice

Every brand has a step where the speaker learns what you sound like. This is called a voice profile and it's how the speaker can tell "call Mom" apart from "call Martin." It takes about 2 minutes and it's worth it.

  • Alexa: App → More → Settings → Your Profile → Alexa Voice ID → follow the prompts.
  • Google: Open the Google Home app and you may be prompted to "Teach your Assistant your voice." If you never see it, tap Settings → your name → Voice Match.
  • Siri: If two or more iPhones are at home, each person turns on "Hey Siri" on their own phone in Settings → Siri & Search. HomePod then recognizes everyone.

Settings that are worth a look on day one

  • Turn Drop-In off on Alexa unless you genuinely love surprise calls from your speaker.
  • Turn off "Allow guests to shop" (see shopping).
  • Set up the speaker's location and hourly weather preferences (Alexa and Google apps ask nicely).

Then say something friendly. "Hey Google, what's the weather?" or "Alexa, tell me a joke." If it answers, congratulations — you've done the whole weekend of work.

One more quick win

Put the speaker app icon somewhere on your phone's first screen. In week three, you'll want to change a timer or a setting, and finding that app in a folder full of games is a meeting you don't want to have.
