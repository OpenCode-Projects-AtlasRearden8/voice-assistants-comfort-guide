Daily Commands That Actually Earn Their Keep

You don't need to memorize a manual. You need about a dozen phrases that are actually more convenient than your phone. Here they are, grouped by what they're for. Brand-specific wording is in brackets so you can copy the exact one.

The everyday arsenal

Time and weather

  • "What time is it?"
  • "What's the weather today?" / "Will it rain tomorrow?"
  • [Alexa: "What's the weather in Chicago?"] for checking on someone you love who lives somewhere cold.

Timers (the #1 use for most people)

  • "Set a timer for 10 minutes."
  • "Set a pasta timer for 9 minutes."
  • "How much time is left on my timer?"
  • "Cancel the timer" / "Cancel all timers."

Reminders

  • "Remind me to call the pharmacy at 3pm."
  • "Remind me to take my meds every day at 8am."
  • "What are my reminders?"

Alarms

  • "Set an alarm for 6:30am."
  • "Set a repeating alarm for 6:30am weekdays."
  • "Snooze." (seriously, this works)

Music and audio

  • "Play some jazz." / "Play the new [artist] album."
  • Alexa: "Play AirTime radio" or "Play [station] on TuneIn."
  • Google: "Play lo-fi beats on YouTube Music."
  • HomePod: things play through Apple Music by default; "Play my morning playlist."
  • "Pause." / "Resume." / "Next song." / "Louder." / "Softer."

Facts and questions

  • "What's 15% of 42 dollars?" / "Convert 3 tablespoons to cups."
  • "How many ounces in a cup?" (cooking, constantly)

Phone stuff while your hands are busy

  • "Call [contact name]." (after voice profile is set up)
  • "What's my battery level?" (Alexa: on some devices)

Cooking trio (worth learning as a set)

  1. "Set a 20-minute timer."
  2. "How many tablespoons in a quarter cup?"
  3. "Play the [recipe] recipe." — Alexa and Google will read or walk you through a recipe step by step, hands-free when your hands are covered in dough. Check your app for "recipe" skills/actions.

Around the house

  • "Turn on the living room light." (needs a smart bulb or plug — see the smart-home guide)
  • "Set the thermostat to 72."
  • "Lock the front door." (if you have a smart lock)
  • "Find my phone." (Alexa/Google — your phone rings even when it's on silent)

The polite stuff

"Please" and "thank you" work fine and cost nothing. It's good for the kids and harmless to the robots.
