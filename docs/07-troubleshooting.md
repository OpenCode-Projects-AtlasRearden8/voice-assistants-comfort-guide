# My Speaker Stopped Listening: The Fix-It Ladder

It responds only to heavy sighing, sometimes. Or not at all. Before you decide it's broken forever, walk this ladder from top to bottom. Each rung takes about a minute and most problems die on rung 3 or 4.

## Rung 1: The obvious stuff (no judgment)

- Is it **plugged in** at both ends (wall *and* speaker)? Power strips get bumped.
- Is the **mic mute button** on? Red light or mic-with-a-line icon means mute. Press it. Try again.
- **Volume.** Plausible, but say "louder" first. Then check app volume.

## Rung 2: Restart properly (the 80% fix)

Unplug the speaker. Count to 30. Plug it back in. Wait 90 seconds for it to boot.

Do this before anything else. It's the IT equivalent of "have you tried turning it off and on again," and it works an embarrassing percentage of the time.

## Rung 3: Wi-Fi is usually the culprit

- If your **phone's Wi-Fi is slow** right now, the speaker's is too.
- The speaker likes the **2.4GHz network** in big houses. If your router broadcasts two networks, try the other one.
- Move the speaker closer to the router for a test. If it suddenly works great in the hallway, the problem is distance/walls, and the fix is a mesh node or a different outlet, not a new speaker.
- Router reboot: unplug router, 30 seconds, plug back in. Yes, again.

## Rung 4: Check if it's *hearing* you (not just *listening*)

- Noisy rooms (dishwasher, TV, kids) cause real problems. Try getting closer and speaking normally, not louder.
- Ambient music playing on the speaker itself can muffle "Hey Alexa." Turn it down and retry.
- Morning-voice problem is real — speak the wake word *clearly*, separate from the rest. "Hey… Alexa." Two beats.
- If you recently changed the wake word (Alexa supports "Echo," "Amazon," "Computer") make sure that's what you're saying.

## Rung 5: Check the app says what you think

- **Device online?** Open the app, tap the speaker, look for "Online" / a green dot.
- **Wrong account?** If the household list changed, the speaker might be registered to an old account. Settings → Device → "Registered to" [name].
- **Do Not Disturb mode:** speakers in DND still respond to timers but won't make noise for reminders. Settings → device → Do Not Disturb.
- **Bluetooth:** if it's paired to your phone, phone audio can mute the assistant. Unpair and retry.

## Rung 6: Deregister and re-setup (nuclear, 10 minutes)

Alexa: app → Devices → select device → Settings → trash icon / "Deregister." Then re-run setup from scratch — it takes less time than it sounds and fixes weird account/voice-profile ghosts. Google: factory reset the speaker via the physical button (varies by model; Google has a page per model), re-add in the Home app.

## Rung 7: It might actually be broken

If it was dropped, soaked, or it stays dark, that's warranty territory. Amazon: order page "Get product support." Google: store page support. Apple: apple.com/support. This guide is not affiliated with any of them; we're just telling you where the door is.

## Cheat answers to the most common real-world cases

- "It stopped hearing me after a power outage" → rung 2 (restart), then rung 3 (Wi-Fi rejoin).
- "It hears me but only sometimes" → rung 4 (mic/noise), then check voice profile exists.
- "It randomly talks/disturbs at night" → DND mode or Quiet hours in the app.
- "My partner's voice gives my reminders" → voice profiles are per-person; each person sets one up separately.
- "Wake word doesn't work after firmware update" → restart, then re-check the wake word setting in the app.

## When all else fails: use your phone

You have a phone in your pocket that can do 90% of this. More on that in [phone vs. speaker](08-phone-vs-speaker.md).
