# Kids and Guests: Settings That Keep the Peace

Two kinds of people will use your speaker without reading this guide: kids, and guests. Here's how to set things up so neither one causes a problem.

## Kids

### Alexa (Echo + Kids)

1. **Make a Kids profile:** Alexa app → More → Settings → **Child Accounts** (Amazon Kids+). Add your kid and their age.
2. On the speaker itself: Settings (device page) → **Amazon Kids** toggle → pick "Enable" and sign in with your Amazon account. Kids speakers (Echo Dot Kids edition) light up in friendly colors.
3. Turn **"Age filters"** on so music, podcasts, and skills won't go PG-13 on you.
4. **Set "Parent approved purchases":** Off, or "Ask for approval" so a kid saying "buy more Naruto" can't just… do it.
5. **Block adult skills:** Alexa app → Parent Dashboard → check "Skills" and revoke anything weird.

### Google (Nest speakers)

1. **Create a Google account for the kid** (ages under 13 get special supervision accounts; this guide can't cover all of it, but google.com/families walks you through).
2. Google Home app → Settings → **Household members** → add the kid's account to your household.
3. Google Home app → tap your speaker icon → Settings → **Digital Wellbeing** (or "Kid-safe features" if a kids-account is in the household) → filter music/services to kid-friendly.
4. **Voice Match:** kids get their own voice profile so the assistant knows whose calendar is whose.
5. Google's **Family Link** app is the parental dial for the kid's Google account overall.

### Apple (HomePod)

Not much built-in supervision for kids yet; make them a separate Apple ID via Family Sharing, and set screen-time/communication limits in **Screen Time** on the iPhone. "Hey Siri" still works for them by voice.

### Universal tips with kids

- **Night mode / Quiet hours:** every brand lets you set hours when the volume drops and alarms don't ring. Set it to the hours you are trying to sleep.
- **Don't put "buy" anywhere near routine trigger words.** Nothing like a routine named "candy" with a shopping action.
- **Explicit music filtering:** Alexa: Settings → Music → Explicit Language Filter. Google: Digital Wellbeing → "Explicit content" off.

## Guests

### Option A: Turn on guest mode (if you're friendly)

- **Alexa "Guest Connect":** Settings → your device → Guest Connect → On. Guests with their own Alexa account can use *their* music and *their* reminders on your speaker after a quick pairing — and nobody else's.
- Apple HomePods handle this automatically: any iPhone that's been near the speaker gets a prompt on its lock screen offering "use HomePod," and their music and Siri follow them for a bit.

### Option B: Don't (also fine)

Keep the household list small. Google Household and Amazon Household both let you *remove* people later (Settings → Household members). If your cousin set up an account on your speaker in 2022, and you forgot, remove that legacy entry — voice profile confusion lives there.

### First-guest checklist

- Tell guests: "Just say 'Hey Google' and if it doesn't work, it's fine, we'll yell together."
- If a guest asked about smart locks, make sure the **door lock passcode** isn't on a piece of paper that says "door lock passcode" next to the door.
- Before long visits: turn **voice purchasing** off entirely (see [shopping](05-shopping.md)), so no children or relatives discover it exists.

## One last thing

Kids think voice commands are magic. Let them have the magic — but keep the **shopping, locks, and doorbell** off-limits until they're old enough to explain the concept of "surprise charges."
