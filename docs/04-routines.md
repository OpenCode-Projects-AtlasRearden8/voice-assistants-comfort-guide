# Routines: Reminders and Timers on Autopilot

A **routine** is a recipe: *when you say X (or when Y happens), do Z.*

The magic of routines is that they collapse ten taps into one sentence. "Good morning" can turn on a light, read the weather, tell you your calendar, and start the coffee maker. Here's how to build your first ones, and which ones are worth the 5 minutes.

## How to make a routine (2 minutes, once you've done one)

### Alexa
1. Alexa app → **More** → **Routines** → the **+** button.
2. **When this happens:** pick Voice (type your trigger phrase) or Schedule (pick a time).
3. **Add action:** what should happen — play music, tell me the weather, set volume to 4, whatever.
4. Save.

### Google Home
1. Google Home app → **Automations (Routines)** → **+**.
2. **Add starter:** either a voice command you'll say, or a time of day.
3. **Add action:** what happens.
4. Save.

### Apple (HomePod)
Siri doesn't have full "routines" the way Alexa/Google do — but you can build them in the iPhone **Shortcuts** app and *run* them by name on your HomePod: make a Shortcut, then "Hey Siri, morning routine."

## The five routines that are actually worth making

1. **"Good morning"** — lights on soft, weather, today's calendar, news briefing, coffee (if it's on a smart plug). This one changes lives.
2. **"Goodnight"** — lights off, doors locked (if smart locks), "do not disturb" mode until 7am. Saying this out loud before bed is weirdly satisfying.
3. **"I'm home"** — lights on, thermostat up, maybe a message to the household. Handy when your arms are full of groceries.
4. **Medication reminder** — a *scheduled* routine (not voice-triggered) at, say, 8am and 8pm: flashlight blink or a spoken "Time for pills." Routines repeat daily automatically.
5. **"Bedtime story" or "wind down"** — timer to remind the kids at a fixed hour, or a 15-minute "timer" that turns off the lamps.

## Scheduled reminders worth setting up right now

Say these out loud right after setup — they take 5 seconds each:

- "Remind me to take the trash out Thursday nights at 7."
- "Remind me to call Mom every Sunday at 4." (or "…every day at 10am" for repeating things)
- "Remind me to pay the electric bill on the 1st of every month."

## Pro tips (the friendly kind)

- **Name timers by what they're for.** "Timer for rice," "timer for laundry." Then "How long on the rice timer?" gives a real answer instead of "there are three timers."
- **Routines are quieter than your phone.** A routine that *speaks* the reminder is better than one that just makes the speaker blink — you won't notice the blink.
- **Test each routine once.** Say the trigger phrase. If two routines accidentally have the same trigger word ("goodnight" and "goodnight kids"), fix it — the speaker gets confused and does the wrong one.
- Keep a paper note of your routine phrases for the first week. After that, they stick.
